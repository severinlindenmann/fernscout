The card

Date, what is in the run, then the ticker — segmented, monospace, tabular figures so nothing shifts as digits change. A hairline bar underneath shows how much of the two-day window is left, which the numbers alone never convey.

Why the bar earns its three pixels

“1 day 20 hours” is a number somebody has to weigh against a window they were never told the size of. The bar says most of it is still there without a sentence, and it is the thing that turns red long before the digits look alarming.

The segments drop away as the clock does

Four segments at two days, three under a day, two under an hour. The ticker gets shorter and louder as it runs out — a shape somebody reads before they read a digit. Only the last minute pulses, and only on the seconds; a card that throbs for two days is a card people close.

When it has run out

The sweep runs when somebody opens an import, not on a timer — so a run genuinely outlives its own deadline and still appears. It must never show a negative, and it must not pretend there is time left.

What the build has to honour

ThingRule
font-variant-numeric: tabular-numsNon-negotiable. Proportional digits make the whole card jitter every second, which is the difference between a ticker and a twitch.
Tick rateSeconds are visible below one hour only. Above that the seconds segment is dropped and the tick drops with it — a list saying 01 : 20 does not need to wake the phone every second.
PulseLast minute only, seconds only, and never under prefers-reduced-motion.
Past zero00 : 00 : 00, dashed, and a line saying it goes at the next sweep. Never a negative, never a stale positive.
ColourTokens only. The coral and yellow here are the dark-aware ones from B1798 — a raw hex would fail in light mode, which this feature has already shipped twice.
Bar widthFraction of the current window, so an extension refills it. Not a fraction of 48 hours.

One thing I would argue against

Flip-clock digits — the little cards that rotate. They look wonderful in isolation and they are a per-frame animation on a list that may hold several runs, on a phone, on a screen whose entire message is “you have time, but not unlimited time”. The tabular jump does that job at a fraction of the cost. If you want the flip, say so and it is yours; I would just rather you chose it knowing what it costs.
