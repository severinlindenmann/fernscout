Somebody has shared their travel journal with you. This page explains how to
read it, how to be told when there is something new, and how to correct your
own details.

You do not need an account, and there is no password anywhere in this.

## Getting in

Everything here works with your email address and a six-digit code. Nothing
else.

![The front page on a phone: a card headed "Has someone shared a travel journal with you?" with a "Sign in to read" button.](/docs/guides/figures/guide-signin-en.webp)

1. Open the site and press **Sign in to read**. It is the first thing on the
   page, above everything else.
2. Type the email address the invitation was sent to, and press **Send me a
   code**.

   ![The sign-in card asking for an email address, with a "Send me a code" button.](/docs/guides/figures/guide-signin-form-en.webp)

3. A code arrives by email. Type it in — or press the button in that email,
   which does the same thing without typing.

   ![The same card, now asking for the six-digit code.](/docs/guides/figures/guide-signin-code-en.webp)

You stay signed in on that device for a year. If you read on a phone and a
laptop, you sign in once on each.

**Signing in does not give you access to anything by itself.** It only proves
which address is yours. What you can then read is whatever the person whose
journal it is has already shared with you — so if you have just been invited
and cannot see anything yet, they may not have approved you.

### If the email does not arrive

Look in your spam folder first. A code lasts thirty minutes; after that, ask
for a new one — the old one stops working the moment a new one is sent, so
always use the newest email.

## What you see after signing in

The front page becomes your own list: every journal on this server you have
been let into, in one place. Each one says how you got in — whether it is
yours, whether you were on the trip, or whether it was shared with you.

![The front page after signing in, listing two journals with badges reading "Yours" and "You travelled".](/docs/guides/figures/guide-home-en.webp)

You can get back to that list at any time from the link above the journal's
name at the top of the page.

## Being told when there is a new day

You can have your phone or computer tell you when a new day is published.
This is off until you turn it on, and it is per journal — turning it on for
one journal says nothing about any other.

### On an iPhone or iPad

Apple only allows this for a site you have added to your Home Screen, so there
is one extra step. It is worth doing once:

1. Open the journal in **Safari** — not Chrome, and not a link inside another
   app.
2. Press the **Share** button — the square with an arrow pointing up. If you
   press and hold the page instead, it is the first entry in the menu.

   ![The Safari menu, with Share as its first entry above Add to Bookmarks.](/docs/guides/figures/ios-share.webp)

3. The sharing panel opens. **Scroll down inside it** — past the apps — and
   choose **Add to Home Screen**, then **Add**. It is near the bottom, which
   is why it is easy to miss.

   ![The sharing panel, scrolled down to a list ending in Add to Home Screen.](/docs/guides/figures/ios-add-home.webp)
4. Close Safari and open the journal again **from the new icon** on your Home
   Screen.
5. Read a little. A card appears at the bottom offering to tell you about new
   days — press **Yes, notify me**, then **Allow** when your phone asks.

If you would rather not wait for the card, the same switch is on your own page
under **Notifications** — see below for how to find it.

![The Notifications section on your own page, with a "Tell me about new days" button, and the card that offers the same thing at the bottom of the screen.](/docs/guides/figures/guide-notify-en.webp)

### On Android, or on a computer

No extra step. Open the journal, read a little, and the same card appears at
the bottom. Or go to your own page and use the switch under **Notifications**.

### Turning it off again

The same switch turns it off. If you would rather not be asked about it at
all, the card has **Don't ask me again**, and it is remembered.

## Your details, and your address

If the journal's owner sends postcards, they need a postal address — and you
may want to correct it, change the language you are written to in, or stop the
emails altogether.

Every email you get from a journal has a link in the footer to **your
details**. That link is all you need: it opens a page with your own record on
it, and no sign-in.

On that page you can:

- correct your name, your address or your phone number,
- choose which language you are written to in,
- turn the emails off,
- or delete your record entirely.

If you have lost every email, sign in and open your own page — there is a link
to the same place from there.

## Your own page

Once signed in, open the journal and press the person icon in the row of icons
at the top. That page tells you what you can see in this journal, lets you
switch notifications on or off, and links to your details.

It is also where you sign out — which ends the sign-in on that device
everywhere on this server, straight away.

## If something does not work

- **You cannot see a trip you were told about.** The person whose journal it
  is has to approve you before a shared trip opens, and some trips are only
  ever visible to the people who were actually on them. Ask them.
- **You are asked to sign in again on a device where you already did.**
  Signing out on one device ends it on that device only; but if you pressed
  **Sign out** or removed the device from your list, you sign in again.
- **The notification card never appears.** Either notifications are switched
  off for that journal, or you have already answered — check the switch on
  your own page.
