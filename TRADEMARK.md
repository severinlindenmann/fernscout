# Trademark policy

**Fernscout**, and the waymark logo in `docs/branding/fernscout-logo.svg`,
are the project's name and mark. This document explains what that does and
doesn't mean for you.

## The short version

The code is free. The name and logo are not part of that.

- **Code:** licensed under PolyForm Shield 1.0.0 (see `LICENSE`). Use it,
  self-host it, modify it, redistribute it, free and for as long as you like —
  with one restriction, which is that you may not use it to provide a product
  that competes with Fernscout.
- **Name and logo:** not covered by that grant either. You can build on the
  code freely; that doesn't hand you the right to call your fork or your
  hosted service "Fernscout", or to use the logo, in a way that suggests it
  *is* Fernscout or is endorsed by this project.

The two are separate restrictions and it is worth keeping them apart: the
licence decides **whether** you may run a competing service, the trademark
decides only **what you may call it**. Renaming does not buy you the first
one.

## What's fine

- Running Fernscout for yourself, your family, or a private group, unmodified
  or modified — no branding question arises, because you're not presenting
  anything to the public as a distinct project.
- Saying your project is "based on Fernscout" or "a fork of Fernscout", in
  plain text, to accurately describe where the code came from.
- Screenshots, blog posts, and comparisons that reference the name.

## What's not fine

- Naming a public fork "Fernscout" (or something confusingly close to it),
  or using the waymark logo for it, in a way that could make people think
  it's this project or affiliated with it.
- A competing hosted service, under any name at all — that one is the
  licence's answer rather than this document's, and rebranding does not
  change it.
- Any use that implies endorsement, partnership, or official status this
  project hasn't actually given you.

If you're unsure whether something you want to do crosses that line, ask —
open an issue on the repository.

## Why "unregistered"

**Fernscout is not a registered trademark.** No filing has been made with
the Swiss IGE, DPMA, or EUIPO. What protection the name has comes from
actual use — Swiss unfair-competition law and common-law rights recognise a
name once it's genuinely in use, but that protection is weaker and less
certain than a registration, and this document does not claim otherwise.
Registration may happen later; until it does, treat this policy as "please
don't do this," backed by the more limited rights unregistered use actually
carries, not as a claim of trademark rights that have been formally secured.
